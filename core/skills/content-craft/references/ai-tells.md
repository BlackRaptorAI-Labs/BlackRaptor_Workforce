# AI tells — human-voice avoid-list (all packs)

The combined avoid-list. Loaded on demand by `content-craft`; not inline in the skill and never in
the Core contract (token discipline). ONE list, applied in every pack. **Refresh date: 2026-08-21 (§2.6 extended, R20)**
— re-pull current tells quarterly (or when the writing agents' output starts showing a new tic); a
frozen list decays (the 2026 tells below were missing from the first draft's pre-2026 sources).

## Two standing rules that travel WITH this list (read first)

1. **Accuracy outranks style.** Keep a flagged word ONLY when it is the factually precise term and
   no honest substitute exists (a "robust" reliability requirement, "leverage" in a finance model, a
   named "landscape" in GIS). This is one rule, not an exception list.
2. **Avoid, do not mechanically purge.** Text scrubbed of every item reads as conspicuously scrubbed
   — its own tell. The target is natural human prose, not a forbidden-word filter.

elevate · resonate · seamless · empower · harness · unlock · synergy · paradigm · cutting-edge ·

## 2.2 Opener tells
"It's important to note (that)…" · "It's worth noting…" · "In today's fast-paced world…" · "In
today's rapidly evolving…" · "In the ever-evolving world of…" · "When it comes to…" · "Picture this /
Imagine a world where…" · "Let me break this down…"

## 2.3 Hedge tells
"That being said…" · "However, it's important to consider…" · "On the other hand…" · "There are
several considerations…" · "It depends on various factors…" · "This is a complex topic with no single
answer…" · "Both sides have valid points…" · "Results may differ…"

## 2.4 Closing tells
"In conclusion…" · "The key takeaway is…" · "Ultimately, the choice is yours…" · "As we move
forward…" · "Only time will tell…" · "The possibilities are endless…" · "At the end of the day…"

## 2.5 Chat-response tells (in-conversation replies, not just documents)
"Great question!" · "Absolutely!" · "You're absolutely right" · "That's a really interesting point" ·
"You raise a valid concern" · "I hope this helps!" · "Happy to help!" · "I'd be happy to help" · "Let
me know if you have any questions!" · "Best of luck on your journey!" · "Happy [verb]-ing!" · the
"Let me…" construction ("Let me explain / break this down / walk you through").

## 2.6 False-candor & false-importance openers (2026 tells — BAN HARD)
"I'll be straight with you" · "let me be honest" · "to be honest / honestly" · "in all honesty" ·
"the honest truth" · "here's the thing" · "here's the deal" · "the truth is…" · "the reality is…" ·
"look,…" · reflexive use of the adjective "honest" · "one honest gap / one honest note / one honest
thing / one honest caveat" · "it's worth getting exact / it's worth getting this right / worth
getting right" · "worth noting" · "to be clear" · "the short version" · "the key thing". A phrase
meant to SIGNAL candor OR importance, repeated every few paragraphs, reads as filler and quietly
erodes credibility — the opposite of its intent. (Extends Claude's base "avoid
honestly/genuinely/straightforward" to the "honest" adjective, the "one honest X" and "worth getting
exact" families, and the straight-with-you family, in every pack. Owner catch 2026-08-21 — these
appeared repeatedly in live oversight output.)

## 2.7 Sentence-construction tells (often a stronger tell than any word)
"not just X, but Y" · "it's not about X, it's about Y" · "it's not X — it's Y" · "X is more than just
Y" · "X is the new Y" · "No X. No Y. Just Z." · "let that sink in" · "and that's a good thing" ·
"that's the beauty of it".

## 2.8 Engagement-bait tells
"let's dive in" · "let's explore" · "let's unpack" · "buckle up" · "here's the kicker" · "here's the
deal" · "the best part?".

## 2.9 Punctuation & formatting tells
- **Em-dash (—): treat as a red flag, not a default connective.** Current-model text overuses it
  heavily; it is now one of the most recognizable tells. Prefer a period, a comma, or a reworded
  sentence; reserve the em-dash for genuine, rare emphasis.
- Over-bolding phrases mid-paragraph for emphasis throughout.
- Reflexive numbered/bullet lists where prose would read more naturally.
- The rule-of-three cadence (three balanced items, every time).
- "Too-perfect" polish — uniform, frictionless prose with no plain sentences.
